### Paper notation vs. project notation

The paper writes `sigma_{-1}(n) = sigma(n)/n` for the abundancy index — exactly our `I(n)`. This note uses `I(n)`. The other notations coincide: `v_p(n)` = p-adic valuation (extended to rationals by `v_p(m/n) = v_p(m) − v_p(n)`, possibly negative), `ω(n)` = number of distinct primes, `o_n(m)` = multiplicative order of m modulo n. "Nonidentical prime factors" = distinct prime factors. The paper also defines `I(p^∞) := p/(p−1)` (the supremum of `I(p^a)`).

## 2. Exact statements (paper's numbering)

### Lemma 1 (classical facts about I)

Let p, q be primes and a, b, m, n positive integers.

- **(a)** If `a < b`, then `(p+1)/p ≤ I(p^a) < I(p^b) < p/(p−1) =: I(p^∞)`.
- **(b)** If `p > q`, then `I(p^a) < I(q^b)` (for any a, b ≥ 1).
- **(c)** If `gcd(m,n) = 1`, then `I(mn) = I(m)·I(n)`.
- **(d)** If `m | n`, then `I(m) ≤ I(n)`.
- **(e)** If `m | n` and `I(m) = I(n)`, then `m = n`.

(Stated without proof in the paper; (d)+(e) together give the strict version we use in the overview (docs/METHODOLOGY.md): a proper divisor has strictly smaller index.)

### Proposition 2 (basic constraints)

Let n be a friend of 10. Then:

- **(a)** n is a perfect square, `5 | n` and `gcd(n, 6) = 1` (that is, n is odd and `3 ∤ n`).
- **(b)** `ω(n) ≥ 5`.

**Proof method:** From `I(n) = 9/5` comes `9n = 5·σ(n)`, hence `5 | n`. If `2 | n`, then `10 | n` with `I(n) = I(10)`, and Lemma 1(e) forces `n = 10`, contradicting the definition of friend — so n is odd. n odd forces `σ(n)` odd; since `σ(n) ≡ ∏(a_j + 1) (mod 2)`, all exponents a_j are even, hence n is a square. To exclude 3: if `3 | n`, the bounds of Lemma 1 force exactly `3^2 || n` and `5^2 || n` (exponents ≥ 4 overshoot 9/5); then `m = n/225` satisfies `I(m) = (9/5)/((13/9)(31/25)) = 405/403`, which forces `403 = 13·31 | m` and hence `I(m) ≥ I(403) > 405/403`, contradiction. Finally, `I(n) < I(5^∞)I(7^∞)I(11^∞)I(13^∞) = (5/4)(7/6)(11/10)(13/12) < 9/5` shows that 4 primes do not suffice.

### Remarks after Proposition 2 — relevant to Phase 3

Unnumbered, but strategically important:

1. For odd perfect numbers the same arguments give the Eulerian form `p^a·m^2` with `p ≡ a ≡ 1 (mod 4)` and `ω ≥ 3`.
2. For friends n of `2p` with p prime, `p ≥ 7` (i.e. `I(n) = (3/2)(p+1)/p`): only weaker conclusions — n is odd, `p | n`, `ω(n) ≥ 2`; and **if** `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, n is a square (no improvement in ω).
3. **Key obstruction identified by the author:** for friends of 10 it is easy to prove `3 ∤ n`; for friends of `2p` with `p ≠ 5`, apparently not. This is why the problem of 10 is especially well suited to computational search, and the analogues 14, 15, 20 etc. are harder.

### Proposition 3 (bounds for the smallest unknown prime)

Let `ℓ₁, k₁, k` be integers with `0 ≤ ℓ₁ ≤ k₁ < k`. Let `n = ∏_{j=1}^{k} p_j^{a_j}` (distinct primes, positive exponents), with the "unknown" primes ordered: `p_{k₁+1} < p_{k₁+2} < … < p_k` (no order is assumed among the first k₁). Let `b_j` be integers with `a_j ≥ b_j` for `ℓ₁+1 ≤ j ≤ k₁`. (In the application: primes `p_1..p_{k₁}` known; exact exponents known only for `p_1..p_{ℓ₁}`; for the others only lower bounds `b_j` are known.) Suppose `t_min ≤ I(n) ≤ t_max` with `t_min, t_max` positive reals. Define

```
m = t_min / ( ∏_{j=1}^{ℓ₁} I(p_j^{a_j}) · ∏_{j=ℓ₁+1}^{k₁} I(p_j^∞) )
M = t_max / ( ∏_{j=1}^{ℓ₁} I(p_j^{a_j}) · ∏_{j=ℓ₁+1}^{k₁} I(p_j^{b_j}) )
```

Then:

- **(a)** `M > 1` and `p_{k₁+1} ≥ 1/(M−1)`.
- **(b)** If `a_{k₁+1} ≥ 2`, then `p_{k₁+1} > B_low := [1/(M−1)] · 8/((2−M)² + 7)`.
- **(c)** If `m > 1`, then `p_{k₁+1} < B_high := 1 + (k−k₁)/(m−1)`.
- **(d)** If `k₁+2 ≤ k`, `a_{k₁+1} ≥ 2` and A is a real with `1 < A ≤ p_{k₁+1}` and `m(A−1)/A > 1`, then `p_{k₁+2} < g(A) := 1 + (k−k₁−1)/( m(A−1)/A − 1 )`.

**Proof method:** adaptation of the classical argument of Cohen–Sorli and Nielsen (2007, section 6). (a) and (c) compare I(n) with telescoping products of `I` over consecutive primes. (b) uses `I(p^2) > (1 + 1/(2p))²` plus the polynomial inequality `sqrt(1+x) ≤ 1 + x/2 − x²/8 + x³/16` (valid for `x ≥ −1`), chosen **deliberately as a rational function of M to keep exact arithmetic on the computer** (instead of `p > 1/(2√M − 2)`); the improvement factor `8/((2−M)²+7)` is `> 1` when `1 < M < 3`. (d) chains (c) and (b).

### Proposition 4 (valuations of σ(x^a) = (x^{a+1}−1)/(x−1); lifting-the-exponent style)

Let a be a positive integer, p an odd prime, x an integer with `x > 1` and `p ∤ x`.

- **(a)** If `p | x−1`, then `v_p((x^{a+1}−1)/(x−1)) = v_p(a+1)`.
- **(b)** If `p ∤ x−1` and `o_p(x) | a+1`, then `v_p((x^{a+1}−1)/(x−1)) = v_p(x^{o_p(x)} − 1) + v_p(a+1)`.
- **(c)** If `o_p(x) ∤ a+1`, then `p ∤ (x^{a+1}−1)/(x−1)`. In particular, if a is even, `p = 5` and `5 ∤ x−1`, then `5 ∤ (x^{a+1}−1)/(x−1)`.

**Proof method:** the binomial theorem gives `v_p(m^p − 1) = v_p(m−1) + 1` and `v_p(m^r − 1) = v_p(m−1)` for `p ∤ r`, when `p | m−1`; combining, `v_p(m^r − 1) = v_p(m−1) + v_p(r)`. Apply with `m = x` (part a) or `m = x^{o_p(x)}` (part b; `o_p(x) | p−1` is coprime to p). The special case in (c): if `5 ∤ x−1` then `o_5(x) ∈ {2,4}` is even, while `a+1` is odd.

### Proposition 5 (prime factors of σ(p^a) with prescribed order)

Let p be an odd prime and a an **even** positive integer. For every divisor d of `a+1` with `d > 1`, the number `σ(p^a)` has a prime factor `q_d` such that `o_{q_d}(p) = d`.

**Proof method:** `(x^{a+1}−1)/(x−1) = ∏_{d | a+1, d>1} Φ_d(x)` (cyclotomic polynomials) and Bang's theorem (1886): `Φ_d(p)` has a prime factor q with order of p mod q equal to d, except in three cases — `(d,p)=(1,2)`, `(d,p)=(6,2)`, or (`d=2` and `p+1` a power of 2) — none of which occurs with d and p odd (a even ⟹ a+1 odd ⟹ every d | a+1 is odd).

### Corollary 6 (NEW according to the author — general exponent bound)

Let n be an odd square with `ω(n) = k > 1`. Let r be a prime factor of `gcd(n, σ(n))`. Let c be a non-negative integer such that

```
Σ_{q prime : q|n, gcd(r, q(q−1)) = 1, o_r(q) | v_q(n)+1}  v_r(q^{o_r(q)} − 1)  ≤  c.
```

Suppose every prime factor q of n with `q > r` satisfies `v_q(I(n)) = 0`. Then

```
v_r(n) ≤ (k−1)² + c − v_r(I(n)).
```

(Caution: `v_r(I(n))` is the valuation of the rational `I(n)` and may be negative — for friends of 10 and r = 5 it equals −1, which adds +1 to the bound.)

**Proof method:** Let `a = v_r(n)`, `a₋₁ = v_r(I(n))`; then `a + a₋₁ = v_r(σ(n)) = v_r(σ(n/r^a))` decomposes by Proposition 4 into contributions `v_r(v_q(n)+1)` (primes q with `o_r(q) | v_q(n)+1`) plus contributions `v_r(q^{o_r(q)}−1)` (bounded by c). By the generalised pigeonhole principle some q has `v_r(v_q(n)+1) ≥ b := ⌈(a + a₋₁ − c)/(k−1)⌉`; if `b ≥ 1`, then `r, r², …, r^b` divide `v_q(n)+1` and Proposition 5 produces b distinct prime factors `q_{r^i}` of `σ(q^{v_q(n)})`, each `≡ 1 (mod r^i)`, hence `> r`, hence (by the hypothesis `v_{q'}(I(n)) = 0` for primes `q' > r`) each divides n. Since these b primes are among the k−1 prime factors of n other than r, `b ≤ k−1`, which gives the bound.

### Corollary 7 (NEW — bound on the exponent of 5 for friends of 10)

For every friend n of 10, if `k = ω(n)`, then **`v_5(n) ≤ (k−1)² + 1`**.

**Proof method:** Corollary 6 with `r = 5` and `c = 0`. The value c = 0 is valid because n is a square (`v_q(n)+1` odd for every q) and, for q with `gcd(5, q(q−1)) = 1`, the order `o_5(q) ∈ {2,4}` is even — the sum is empty. Note: `v_5(I(n)) = v_5(9/5) = −1` and `v_5(n) ≥ 2` (5 | n and n a square). Instances used: `k = 8 ⟹ v_5(n) ≤ 50`; `k = 9 ⟹ v_5(n) ≤ 65` (since v_5 is even, ≤ 64).

### Lemma 8 (classical; equivalent to [Nielsen 2007, Lemma 11])

Let p be an odd prime, a a positive integer, y an integer coprime to p. The equation `z^{p−1} ≡ 1 (mod p^a)` has exactly one solution z with `z ≡ y (mod p)`, and that solution satisfies `z ≡ y^{p^{a−1}} (mod p^a)`.

**Proof method:** Hensel applied to `X^{p−1} − 1 ≡ ∏_{ỹ=1}^{p−1}(X − ỹ) (mod p)`; the explicit formula follows from Proposition 4(a).

### Proposition 9 (finite computational verification; slight improvement of [Nielsen 2007, Lemma 12] in fewer cases)

- If x is an integer coprime to 31 and `1 < x ≤ 31^14`, then
  `v_31(x^{o_31(x)} − 1) ≤ v_31(x^30 − 1) ≤ ⌈log_31 x⌉ + 1`.
- If x is an integer coprime to 19531 and `1 < x ≤ 19531^6`, then
  `v_19531(x^{o_19531(x)} − 1) ≤ v_19531(x^19530 − 1) ≤ ⌈log_19531 x⌉ + 1`.

**Proof method:** by Lemma 8, to find x coprime to p in `(p^{a−1}, p^a]` with `v_p(x^{p−1} − 1) ≥ a` it suffices to check the numbers `y^{p^{a−1}} mod p^a` for `y ∈ {2, …, p−1}`. Computer check for `p = 31, a ≤ 15` and `p = 19531, a ≤ 7`. (Relevance: `31 = σ(5²)` and `19531 = σ(5⁶)` are primes; if `v_5(n) = 2` then `31 | n`, and if `v_5(n) = 6` then `19531 | n`.)

### Theorem 10 (main result)

**Every friend of 10 has at least 10 distinct prime factors.** That is: if `N ≠ 10` and `5·σ(N) = 9·N`, then `ω(N) ≥ 10`.

**Proof method:** depth-first search (DFS) over "factor chains" (Nielsen's 2007 scheme, adapted), implemented in SageMath, with `t_min = t_max = 9/5`, `S_ignore = (3)` and exact-arithmetic pruning via Proposition 3. The runs with `5 ≤ k ≤ 7` directly eliminate `ω(n) ∈ {5,6,7}`; the runs with `k ∈ {8, 9}` use Corollaries 6 and 7 to bound `v_5(n)` (and, at two bottlenecks, the "special prime" r with Proposition 9) and eliminate `ω(n) ∈ {8, 9}`. No run produced a candidate partial factorisation; together with Proposition 2 (ω ≥ 5), ω ≥ 10 follows.

## 3. The computer programme (section 3 of the paper) — reusable details

Central propagation idea ("factor chain"): if `v_p(n) = a > 0` and q is a prime factor of `σ(p^a)` with `v_q(I(n)) ≤ 0`, then `v_q(n) ≥ v_q(σ(n)) > 0`, hence `q | n`. State of each tree node:

- `S_on` = "on" primes (confirmed prime factor with **finalised** exponent: either exact `v_q(n) = a_q`, or minimum `v_q(n) ≥ b_q`);
- `S_off` = primes confirmed as divisors but with exponent not yet finalised (`v_q(n) ≥ b_q`); they are by-products of the on-primes;
- `P` = bound: every prime factor outside `S_on ∪ S_off` is `> P`;
- `S_ignore` = primes p with `v_p(I(n)) > 0` (for 10: only 3, since `I(n) = 9/5 = 3²/5`) — factors 3 of `σ(p^a)` do not force `3 | n`;
- `B` = cut-off above which `p^a` is "large" (there the exponent becomes only a lower bound and the branch continues with `b_p`).

Pruning at each node: `M < 1` ⟹ I too large, prune; `m > 1` with no possible primes in the interval `(max{P, B_low}, B_high)` ⟹ prune; upon completing k primes, final tests including violation of Corollary 6. Even exponents only (n is a square). Run parameters: `P = 4` for `k ≤ 7` and `P = 5` for `k ≥ 8`.

Special prime r (used only in 2 runs, with `(r, log_r L, δ) = (31, 14, 1)` and `(19531, 6, 1)`): at the last two levels of the tree, instead of factoring, Proposition 9 is used (via the data `L`, `δ` such that `1 < x ≤ L` coprime to r implies `v_r(x^{o_r(x)}−1) ≤ ⌈log_r x⌉ + δ`) to overestimate the `c` of Corollary 6 and prune when `a_r`/`b_r > (k−1)² + s` is too large (here r = 31 or 19531).

Cases and times (Table 1; total ≈ 25 h of CPU on an i5-1235U laptop, SageMath 10.0):

| k | Start | CPU time |
|---|-------|----------|
| 5, 6, 7 | S_on empty, B = 10³/10⁷/10¹⁴ | < 1 s each |

| 9 | (5, 31), a_5 = 2, a_31 = a ≤ 94 | 7105 s |

External dependencies of the proof (important for reproduction): Cunningham Project tables (for even `a ≤ 124`, `σ(5^a)` has ≥ 2 distinct prime factors, **except** `a ∈ {2, 6, 10, 12, 46}`, when `σ(5^a)` is prime) and FactorDB for the smallest prime factors `q = 316636168836007` of `σ(19531^58)` and `q = 57276919728938572349117407` of `σ(19531^72)` (composites without a factor < 2³¹). These inputs are independently verifiable (divisibility + primality of q).

Cost remarks (section 5): without Corollary 7, `k = 8` took 28500 s and `k = 9` would be "futile". The five cases `v_5(n) ∈ {2, 6, 10, 12, 46}` (exactly those with `σ(5^{v_5})` prime) are the bottlenecks and would be the dominant obstacle to extending to `10 ≤ ω(n) ≤ 12`; for `v_5(n) = 46` one would need an analogue of Proposition 9 for the 33-digit prime `σ(5^46)`, infeasible by direct residue checking.

## 5. Use in the project

### Results to incorporate now (Phases 0/1)

1. **Corollary 7 as an explicit constraint:** `v_5(N) ≤ (ω(N)−1)² + 1` for every friend N of 10. With ω(N) ≥ 10 this gives NO absolute bound (the bound grows with ω), but in any search stratified by ω it prunes the exponent of 5. Re-derive with our own proof (it is short: Prop. 4 + Prop. 5/Bang + pigeonhole) and add to RESULTS.md with a label and a numerical test.
2. **Proposition 2 re-derived** coincides with Ward's constraints already listed (odd, square, 25|N via 5|N + square, 3∤N, least prime 5) — a good cross-check target for Phase 0.
3. **Propagation facts:** `v_5(N) = 2 ⟹ 31 | N`; `v_5(N) = 6 ⟹ 19531 | N`. Useful special cases in any case analysis.

### Reusable techniques (Phase 1 — replicate and extend toward ω ≥ 11)

- **Proposition 3 is the heart of the pruning** and was designed for exact arithmetic (the bound B_low is a rational function of M precisely to avoid square roots) — a perfect fit with the project's "Fraction always" rule. Implement as a utility in `core/` with tests.
- The architecture on/off primes + S_ignore + bound B + even exponents is a complete, reproducible specification of the DFS (section 3 has detailed pseudocode; the SageMath code is in the arXiv supplementary files — worth downloading and auditing before reimplementing).
- **Map of the bottlenecks for ω ≥ 11 already given by the author:** the cases `v_5(N) ∈ {2, 6, 10, 12, 46}` (σ(5^a) prime). Attacking them would require analogues of Proposition 9 for larger special primes; for `σ(5^46)` (33 digits) the direct-check method is infeasible — **this is a point where new theory (valuation bounds without exhaustive search) would have direct impact**. Also worth exploring the referee's observation (section 5): the tree branches for v_5 ∈ {10, 12, 46} are largely common, suggesting shared computation.

### Phase 2 (new structural constraints)

- **Corollary 6 is a general tool**, not specific to 10: it holds for any odd square n and any prime `r | gcd(n, σ(n))` (with the hypothesis `v_q(I(n)) = 0` for primes `q > r` dividing n — automatic for friends of 10 when `r ≥ 5`). Try applying it with other r (other primes guaranteed in N, e.g. the primes ≡ 1 mod 10 / mod 6 of constraint #3) to obtain additional exponent bounds; the cost is estimating the corresponding `c` (for r = 5 the trick was: even orders mod 5 × odd exponents+1 ⟹ c = 0; for other r this fails in general and requires control of `v_r(q^{o_r(q)}−1)`).

### Phase 3 (the 2p family)

- The Remarks after Proposition 2 are the clearest diagnosis in the literature of **why 10 is special**: the easy exclusion of `3 | n`. Any uniform theorem for 2p must either bypass or settle the case `3 | n` — record this as the central obstruction of Phase 3. Structured sub-case: for `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, friends of 2p are squares (same parity of σ), which already gives access to Corollary 6 in that sub-family.

- The result is a computer-assisted proof: to cite it as an external `[PROVED]`, treat it as reliable (peer-reviewed, Indag.
